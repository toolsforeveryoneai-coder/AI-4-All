A big model that fits on the PC you own

Edition of 2026-10-05

# AI For All ### Monday, October 5, 2026

As of today, a small business owner who already owns a gaming PC can run a capable AI model on that machine, with the data never leaving the building and no per-query bill.

What needed a server now fits under a desk

A free, open-source program called Strata runs a large AI model on an ordinary gaming PC, and the current release landed on Sunday. The model is Qwen3.8-Flash-Next. It normally lives on a rented server.

Strata splits it across the parts the machine already has. The graphics card holds the pieces asked for most, system memory holds all of them, and the processor and disk cover the rest. The installer is one click on Windows and Linux.

The floor is honest, and worth reading before you get excited. The graphics card needs 12 GB of video memory. The machine needs 32 GB of system memory, and 64 GB runs every size. Apple machines and Intel cards are not supported at all.

The bug hunter you can keep in your own building

Cantina Security published its first open-weights model on Sunday. It is built with a partner called Yeta, and it is a fine-tune of GLM-5.3-Flash made for one job: read code, chase a flaw, and confirm the effect.

The weights are downloadable. In the firm's own held-out test of 60 tasks, the model solved 66.7% where a frontier model solved 71.7%. That test has not been reproduced outside the firm, so treat the scoreboard as a claim and the download as the fact.

An agent that says it is done, and a record that says otherwise

On Saturday Microsoft and Hugging Face published a test for business agents that ignores what the agent says and checks what it wrote into the system. Across 121,680 valid trials, 79,853 attempts failed the checks, and 67.24% of those failures still ended cleanly, with no error and no sign of trouble. Two thirds of the time an agent got it wrong, it looked to the outside like it had got it right.

That is the number worth keeping. If you have put an agent on customer records, the question is not how it sounds. It is whether the ticket it closed is actually closed.

Weak signal

President Trump announced a "Super Intelligence Force" on Sunday, a White House task force to coordinate federal AI policy, led by the director of national intelligence, Jay Clayton. It follows a September executive order that tells agencies to say "Super Intelligence" in place of "Artificial Intelligence".

It carries reach, not use. Nothing in it changes what you can do today, and the announcement sits on a social account rather than a page this desk can read. Watch it the way you would watch a referee take the field for a market this size.

The weekend's theme was custody. A server-sized model moved onto a PC, a bug hunter moved in-house, and the test that matters moved from what a system says to what it leaves behind.
