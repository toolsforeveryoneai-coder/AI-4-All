Meta's best open model is a download, not a subscription

Edition of 2026-10-16

# AI For All ### Friday, October 16, 2026

As of today, a compliance officer at a bank or a hospital can have an agent work over confidential documents on machines the organisation owns, with the files never leaving the building.

The best American open model is a download, not a subscription

Meta released the weights of Muse Spark Open, and calls it the most capable open-weight model from a US lab to date. You can download it, keep it and run it without asking Meta for permission. This is the second move in a week. Days ago Meta kept its frontier model closed, behind its own API. Now the weights go out.

The machine is where the freedom ends. Meta says this is the first model in its class to run on a desktop, and the desktop it means is two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios working as one computer. Each of those machines carries half a terabyte of memory. This is a filing cabinet you own, and it costs like two cars. The company says so, plainly, rather than letting you find out.

The licence is the part a buyer actually signs. The weights ship under the Muse Model License, with limited commercial terms that let many developers use the model for free, and several hosted providers already serve it, so a company can start without buying any hardware at all.

NVIDIA opened a whole agent family on the same day

NVIDIA released Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, a large open model built for agents that work all day on a company's own servers, plus two smaller edge models. The larger two are downloadable today, the smallest follows on October 23, and the weights, the datasets and the training recipes all ship open.

The claim to hold lightly is speed. NVIDIA says Super VL is five times faster than the next best open model of its class. That is the vendor's own number, and no independent run has priced the model against a rival on the same job yet.

What a buyer can act on is control. Because the weights and the data are open, an organisation can train the model on its own records and run it wherever its rules require. NVIDIA names insurers, banks and hospitals, and lists Scale AI, Perplexity, CrowdStrike and Salesforce among the companies building on it.

The cart now follows you from the search engine to the chatbot

Google's Universal Cart rolled out in the United States, inside Google Search and inside the Gemini app. It is one cart across stores: save a bag in Search, save a sweater in the Gemini app, and both wait in the same place when you return. The cart is built on more than sixty billion product listings that Google keeps refreshed in its Shopping Graph.

Two features make it more than storage. The cart watches a shopper's whole list for price drops and restocks, and it reads the perks on the cards held in Google Wallet to suggest which one to pay with. Checkout runs on Google's Universal Commerce Protocol and works with merchants including Nike, Sephora, Target, Ulta Beauty, Walmart and Wayfair.

The merchant keeps its place in the chain. Google says the store remains the seller of record, the confirmation and the support come from the store, and the shopper can hand the basket back to the retailer's own site to finish. For anyone who sells online, that is the part to read twice: their prices now sit in a cart that compares them against every rival on the same list.

Weak signal

Google DeepMind showed an agent doing the first pass of antibiotic discovery inside Google Antigravity, its developer platform. A biologist and the agent worked together over UniProt, the public protein database, and surfaced a candidate antimicrobial peptide that is now in a lab for testing. The interface is the interesting part: a three-dimensional model of the protein sits inside the same workspace, and the researcher can point at one region and ask the agent to reason about that exact spot. It stays a signal because nothing in it is available today. It is a demonstrated workflow, the lead is unvalidated, and the whole thing runs inside one company's tool.

The strongest model an American lab has built is now a file you can own. The next question is not who can build it, but who can afford to keep it running.
