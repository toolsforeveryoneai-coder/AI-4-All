Nvidia tells customers AI servers cost more next year

Edition of 2026-08-23

# AI For All ### Sunday, August 23, 2026

An IT buyer or ops manager renting AI computing capacity from Microsoft, Google, or Oracle now has to rebuild next year's infrastructure budget around a cost increase of more than fifteen percent, because Nvidia has told its biggest customers that server prices are rising.

The bill for the AI boom just went up, and it's not the chip

Nvidia has warned some of its largest customers that servers built around its AI chips will cost *more than 15%* more in many cases. The increase lands on systems shipping early next year, including the company's flagship Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell platforms. The culprit isn't the processor itself: it's the memory sitting next to it.

That detail matters. Companies that assemble servers under contract for Microsoft, Google, and Oracle have already passed the warning down to their own customers, which means the increase is already working its way through the cloud supply chain before a single server ships. Nvidia can absorb a lot of cost pressure elsewhere: its gross margin sits at *75%*, wide enough that raising prices here is a choice about who eats the shortage, not a sign the company is struggling.

For anyone budgeting cloud AI spend for 2027, that fifteen percent isn't a rounding error. It's the kind of number that reopens a build-versus-rent conversation that looked settled a quarter ago. Nvidia declined to comment when asked by two separate outlets, which tells you this wasn't meant to be public yet.
