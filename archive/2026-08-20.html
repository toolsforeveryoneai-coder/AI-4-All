Your remote, your coding tool, both got cheaper today

Edition of 2026-08-20

# AI For All ### Thursday, August 20, 2026

As of today, anyone with a compatible Fire TV device gets Amazon's full conversational AI assistant free, permanently, without paying the old nineteen-dollar-a-month fee or holding a Prime membership.

Your TV remote just got a raise

Amazon is switching on Alexa+ for every compatible Fire TV in the US today, no signup, no download, no Prime required. If your device qualifies, it just works starting now.

The numbers behind the push: when Alexa+ suggests something to watch, people pick that first recommendation over 40% more than before. That is part of Amazon's own case for why a paywall it built two years ago is coming down.

The list is broad but not universal: current-generation Fire TV Sticks, the Fire TV Cube, Amazon's own smart TVs, and partner sets from Hisense and Panasonic. An older Fire TV Stick that never got the hardware refresh stays exactly where it was. Amazon

What people applaud is not what people pay for

Replit just made its $20-a-month coding plan go a lot further, without raising the price. A new Free Mode gives subscribers up to 30 hours a month of building time that no longer burns their usage credits at all.

The mechanism is unglamorous and worth knowing: OpenAI cut the price of the model underneath, GPT-5.6 Luna, by 80% on July 30, and Replit passed the saving through as more room to build rather than as a new feature to sell. Harder tasks still get routed automatically to a pricier, stronger model, then fall back once done, with the free hours resetting every five hours.

Someone paying twenty dollars a month to turn ideas into working software without writing code now gets a meaningfully bigger monthly allowance for that same twenty dollars. That is a real change in what a subscription is worth, not a new subscription tier. Replit

One week's notice before the ads arrive

ChatGPT will start showing ads to free and low-tier users across 31 European countries starting next Monday, August 24, including Germany, France, Spain and Italy. It follows earlier rollouts in the US, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, the UK, Mexico, Brazil, Japan and South Korea, starting with the US pilot in February.

Europe took longer than the rest of the rollout because GDPR requires explicit opt-in consent for personalized targeting, not the assumed consent OpenAI could rely on elsewhere. The company frames that as a deliberate, slower build rather than a delay it was forced into.

If a free or Go-tier ChatGPT user in Europe wants to avoid ads entirely, the only lever is upgrading past the ad-supported tiers before Monday. There is no opt-out inside the free product itself. Dataconomy

A quiz generator moved into the search box

Google added AI study tools directly into Search and Gemini this week. Type a prompt like "make me a quiz on this week's vocabulary" into ordinary Search, and it now builds one on the spot. Upload a photo of handwritten notes alongside a lecture slide deck, and Search can turn both into a one-page study sheet.

Not everything shown is live yet. A step-by-step Lens helper and new 3D simulations inside Gemini are still described as arriving "in the coming weeks." The quiz generator and the study-document builder are the parts a student can actually use today. TechCrunch

Weak signal

TechCrunch reports OpenAI revoked a research group's access to a limited cybersecurity testing program, and separately previewed new privacy commitments due to take effect in September. Neither has a verifiable figure yet, but both are worth a follow-up once the details land.

Four products got measurably better value overnight without a single new model name attached to any of them. That is worth watching more than the next benchmark chart.
