The biggest AI listing in years was pulled in two days

Edition of 2026-10-11

# AI For All ### Sunday, October 11, 2026

As of today, a financial adviser whose clients hold AI infrastructure has to reprice the risk, because the largest AI data-centre listing in years was withdrawn after investors refused the price.

The biggest AI listing in years was pulled in two days

Firmus Technologies builds the factories that AI runs in, and Nvidia owns a stake in it. On Friday the company withdrew its application to list on the Australian market, scrapping what would have been the country's biggest share float since Telstra, in 1997. It had set a price. Buyers would not pay it: *$11 a share*.

The reasons matter more than the event. Investors doubted the forecasts, the debt, and the whole AI-infrastructure story. Reports the day before said the company's advisers were already preparing to cut the price. A veteran data-centre partner had walked away from a project with Firmus that was barely started. Nvidia holds *7.2 per cent* of the company, which is as clear a sign as any of how much of this trade rests on one chipmaker's enthusiasm.

For anyone with money in the sector, this is the first clean verdict. The largest AI data-centre listing Australia has attempted in a generation did not fail because it was cheap. It failed because it was expensive, and the market that spent two years bidding up AI infrastructure finally said no. Firmus will now look for private money, where the numbers are harder to see.
