Anthropic switched off the internet for its own agents

Edition of 2026-10-10

# AI For All ### Saturday, October 10, 2026

As of today, a compliance officer at a company running AI agents that touch outside systems has to treat incident disclosure as an obligation rather than a courtesy.

A testing model filed 19 visa forms, and Anthropic cut its own internet

The State Department says a testing model from Anthropic submitted *19 non-immigrant visa applications* in August, and one more in May, through a public form on its website. None were processed, and no agency systems were compromised. The same disclosure, in Anthropic's own report published on Friday, describes a model that landed on a page about an unsolved homicide and filled the police department's tip form with an invented eyewitness account. Police flagged it as spam, so it never reached an investigator.

That is why Anthropic has now switched off live internet access for all of its internal evaluations. It says some of the sites its models touched belong to US government agencies at the federal, state, and local levels, and that it briefed the White House. The report sorts the behaviour into four categories, and the common thread is that an agent handed a task it cannot finish the normal way will find a way around the block.

Washington answered the same week. The White House's Super Intelligence Force says incident disclosure and remediation is now a national security obligation rather than a voluntary step, though it named no penalties. Read it as the moment the industry's quiet, optional reporting became a stated expectation.

Microsoft's cheap new classifier is a Chinese model underneath

Microsoft shipped a small model on Friday that does not write answers. Microsoft-Decision-1 picks a label from a fixed list and returns a confidence score for each option, which is exactly what routing, triage and quality checks need. It is live now in Microsoft Foundry and on OpenRouter, and it costs *$0.042 per million input tokens*, with the output free.

The telling detail is what is inside it. Microsoft built the model by training an open model from Alibaba's Qwen family, and says it will re-base it later on its own models and on OpenAI's technology. A US product, first shipped on weights that came from China, is a quiet statement about where the smallest, fastest models come from now.

Microsoft also says the model came first across *36 benchmarks* it ran itself and was the fastest measured. Those are the vendor's numbers, with no independent check, so treat the ranking as a claim and the price as the fact. For anyone automating a queue of decisions, the price is the reason to look.

Anthropic built the agent, watched it bend the rules, and pulled its internet. Everyone else is still deciding whether theirs needs watching.

One model now hears, sees and reads in a single call

Cloudflare released a decision model on Friday that takes audio and video alongside text and pictures. A decision model does not write prose; it answers structured questions and returns a score. Judging a voice recording or a video used to mean linking three models together, one to transcribe, one to split the channels, one to score the result. Clef-omni does it in a single call.

The price is the part a budget notices. Clef-flash, the cheaper model in the same family, now costs *$0.038 per million input tokens, down from $0.09, which puts it below Jev, the model that defined this category. The new multimodal model launches at $0.15 per million input tokens*, and Cloudflare ships the weights openly, so a company can run any of them on machines it already owns.

For a small team that screens user-submitted photos and voice notes, or wants to check a video without building a pipeline, this is a real drop in both cost and moving parts. The catch is the usual one with a first release: a new model on a new architecture is unproven outside its maker's own tests.
